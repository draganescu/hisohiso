what Chat Control 1.0 actually is.

it's a temporary derogation from the EU's ePrivacy rules. those rules normally forbid providers from reading the content of your messages. the derogation carves out an exception: services that can already see your message content — think webmail, or a chat app that holds the plaintext — may voluntarily scan it for known child sexual abuse material and grooming. the European Parliament adopted it on 10 july 2026, and it now runs until 3 april 2028.

  • it is voluntary, not an order. it permits scanning; it does not command every provider to scan.
  • it is a stopgap. it keeps the existing voluntary scanning legal while the bigger fight drags on.
  • it is not the scary one. the mandatory-scanning law is a different bill — see below.

the vote that passed by failing.

under second-reading rules, killing the text needs an absolute majority — 361 votes, not just more nos than yeses. 314 MEPs voted to reject it. that was a majority of the room, but 47 votes short of the bar. so the law survived not because a majority backed it, but because not quite enough voted to bury it.

the headline

it passed.

Chat Control 1.0 is now EU law and stays in force until april 2028.

the footnote

nobody won it.

more MEPs present voted against than for. it lived on a procedural threshold, not a mandate.

end-to-end encryption is left out of it.

an amendment adopted alongside the main vote keeps end-to-end encrypted services outside the derogation's scope. that's the honest, un-dramatic reading: this law lets providers who can already read your messages keep scanning them. it doesn't order an encrypted service to scan, and it doesn't ask anyone to weaken or backdoor encryption. a service that literally cannot read your messages has nothing to hand over.

  • if a provider can read it, it may scan it. that's the whole mechanism — access to plaintext.
  • if a provider can't read it, there's nothing to scan. encryption isn't broken; it's simply out of scope.
  • the mandatory version is still not law. the pressure on encryption lives in Chat Control 2.0, which hasn't passed.

don't confuse it with Chat Control 2.0.

the one people fear is the CSA Regulation — "Chat Control 2.0" — a permanent law that would allow detection orders and reach toward client-side scanning: checking your messages on your own device before they're encrypted. that bill has not passed. round after round of trilogue negotiations between Parliament, Council, and Commission have stalled, mostly over exactly this question of scanning everyone without suspicion. 1.0 is the temporary patch; 2.0 is the unresolved argument.

where a room with no memory already stands.

hisohiso wasn't built as a reaction to this vote — it just happens to sit on the safe side of it. a room's secret lives in the link, in the fragment after the # that never leaves your browser. the router only ever sees ciphertext. and when you close the room, there's nothing left to keep, scan, or subpoena — because nothing was stored to begin with.

what a scanner needs

readable content.

every scanning regime — voluntary or mandatory — depends on someone, somewhere, being able to read the message.

what we keep

nothing.

no account, no phone number, no history on our server. close the room and it's gone. there is no plaintext for anyone to reach for.

the law moved. the quiet room didn't have to.

whatever Brussels decides next, the design is the same: a private, accountless line where the secret stays in the link and the server only sees noise. open one, use it, close it — and there's nothing left behind.
